# 1: My early years of College
## Setup
Imagine you are a college student taking a data structures class. You know java well enough to make endodoi (look it up, I had to as well) and still kind of suck at recursion. You look in front of you to see a professor that seems to know what he's talking about, but look down and see a laptop with discord and so many escapes to distract yourself from boring content. This is what being in some computer science lectures was like for me back in the day. There were times that I almost broke out laughing since my friends messaged me something hilarious, but I had to keep it together since there was a whole class going on around me. The days of fun and games were in their prime and I was living the life. I liked my classes to an extent, but I'm a professional procrastinator and find myself getting easily distracted by anything that isn't the lecture itself. It was in this class that I would find out how cool AI was at doing my work, but also how much I was missing because of it.

The first couple assignments were things I've done before: implement a stack, a queue, some linked list shenanegans. All of this was pretty easy, but this was also the time where I discovered Github Copilot. My brother held it in high regard since it could make something called a "red black tree" with ease without the programmer doing pretty much anything. At the time, I had no idea what a red black tree was, but I was intrigued by the fact that an AI could just make it for me. So I tried it out on my first assignment and it worked like a charm. The code worked and I understood what it was doing and figured it was good. One submission later and I got back the expected 100% from the autograder. This wasn't particularly hard to achieve especially on an assignment like this. Now that it was stuck around in my IDE, it poked around here and there and I found myself using it more and more. With the context of the tiny example code bases I was working in, it had no problem linking together the pieces to solve these simple problems. As the material got more complex, I began unknowingly at the time relying on it to understand the content for me. This was especially apparent on the elusive Binary Search Tree.

As the assignment went out, I took a look and brushed it off like it was nothing. I paid attention in class a little and I had been doing fine so far, so no worries, but when it was time to implement, I felt this odd feeling as copilot took the reigns. I knew it was probably doing the right thing and the tests were passing, but I didn't fully understand what was going on. I had no idea how to implement a binary search tree, but copilot did.
